The Science Behind JetLagPro

How circadian rhythms and Chinese medicine inform our approach.

Jet lag isn't just tiredness—it's your body's internal clock fighting against a new time zone.

What Does Science Tell Us?

  • We all have our own circadian rhythm - our personal 24-hour clock.
  • This clock tells us when to feel sleepy, when to be alert, and regulates many other body functions.
  • Even our individual cells have their own timekeepers.
  • Recent research suggests acupuncture and acupressure may influence the body's internal clock.

A master clock and many local clocks

A small cluster of neurons in the brain, the suprachiasmatic nucleus (SCN), acts as the master clock. Light reaching the eyes keeps it set to the outside day. The SCN in turn coordinates clocks in organs such as the liver and heart, each running its own daily rhythm. Inside every cell, a set of clock genes (Clock, Bmal1, Period, and Cryptochrome) switches on and off in a roughly 24-hour loop.

When you fly across several time zones, these clocks do not reset together. The master clock and the organ clocks catch up at different speeds. Left alone, the body usually adjusts by about one hour per day after flying east, and a little faster after flying west. Crossing many time zones can therefore mean several days of poor sleep, low alertness, and feeling unwell while the clocks fall back into step.

Light is the strongest signal, not the only one

Researchers call the signals that set the body clock zeitgebers, German for "time givers." Light is the most powerful one. Others include meal timing, physical activity, and temperature. These non-light cues can shift the master clock and organ clocks in both animal and human studies.

Standard jet lag advice works through these signals: timed light exposure, melatonin, and shifting sleep and meals toward the new schedule. Each can help, but each is hard to time correctly during and right after a long flight. Repeated, timed stimulation of the body is a plausible additional cue. It has not yet been studied systematically in travelers, which is part of why JetLagPro collects research data.

A two-hour schedule from Chinese medicine

Chinese medicine has understood our body's natural rhythms for thousands of years. They discovered that different systems in our body are most active during specific two-hour periods throughout the day. JetLagPro leverages that concept into an accessible technique anyone can use.

Texts dating to the Han Dynasty (200 BCE to 200 CE) describe a 24-hour cycle in which each organ system and its channel has a two-hour period of peak activity. More than 40 years ago, acupuncturists Khoe and later Amaro proposed using that cycle for travel: stimulate the point for each two-hour window on the destination clock instead of the home clock. They used the horary points, one traditional point per channel that is considered most influential during that channel's window.

JetLagPro follows the same idea. The app converts the twelve windows to your destination time and reminds you which point is active. Five classical points sit on the feet or behind the knees, which are hard to reach in an airline seat. For those windows the app can show a more accessible point on the same channel, so the window is not skipped.

What the research shows so far

A 2024 review by Wu and Zhao reported that acupuncture can affect clock genes, brain signaling chemicals, and sleep and wake regulation. Those studies mostly used points chosen for sleep, not the travel schedule, but they support the idea that acupuncture may act as a non-light timing cue for the body clock.

Timed acupressure for jet lag has been recommended by practitioners for decades but has not been measured in large groups of real travelers. JetLagPro is part of a pre-registered observational study that compares symptoms in travelers who follow the schedule closely with those who use it less. The study is designed to generate hypotheses for future controlled trials, not to prove that the method works. Read the methods and early results in the research paper.

JetLagPro is instructional wellness support, not a medical device or a substitute for professional care. If you have a medical condition or take medications that affect sleep, talk with your clinician before starting a new travel routine.
